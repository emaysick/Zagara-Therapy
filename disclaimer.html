Website information

A few important things to know.

Please read this information about the limits of website content and online inquiries.

Website information only

The information on this website is provided for general educational and informational purposes. It is not mental health treatment, diagnosis, or individualized clinical advice, and it should not be used as a substitute for care from a qualified professional who understands your circumstances.

No therapist-client relationship

Viewing this website, using its information, or emailing the practice does not by itself create a therapist-client relationship. A relationship is established only after the practice and prospective client have completed the appropriate intake and consent process and agreed to begin services.

Services and location

Zagara Therapy offers online psychotherapy to adults who are located in Colorado for sessions, subject to clinical appropriateness, availability, and applicable laws and professional requirements. Website information is not a guarantee that services will be available or appropriate for every person.

Email and response times

Email is intended for general inquiries and scheduling-related communication. It may not be secure, and it is not monitored as an emergency service. Please do not send detailed sensitive health information through ordinary email. Response times may vary.

Emergencies and crisis support

This website and its email address are not monitored continuously and are not appropriate for urgent or emergency support. If you are in immediate danger or need emergency assistance, call 911 or go to the nearest emergency department. In the United States, you can call or text 988 to reach the Suicide & Crisis Lifeline.

External websites

Links to outside websites are provided for convenience. Zagara Therapy does not control and is not responsible for the content, availability, privacy practices, or services of third-party sites.

Fees and estimates

Website descriptions of services and fees are informational and may be updated. Prospective clients may request a written Good Faith Estimate of expected charges when applicable. Under federal rules, providers generally must provide a Good Faith Estimate to people who do not have insurance or do not plan to use insurance when they request one or schedule services at least three business days in advance. An estimate is not a bill or a contract. Learn more from the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services.
